Field advisory: two new ways to die

Some of them are rigged. There are exploding infected on the island. You'll almost always meet them as NBC infected with a gas tank strapped to their back. Drop them at range. Not in a doorway, not in a stairwell, not in the back of a truck. The blast does not care whose side you are on.

STAG left something behind. Somebody high up in the STAG program had an island chain to play with and nobody left alive to tell them no. The outer islands are not empty any more. They are stocked.

  • The mainland is clear, with one exception: the mountaintop at the STAG bunker entrance. Treat that approach as hostile ground.
  • The south-eastern islands are the problem. Go loaded, go in daylight, and go with somebody who will notice you stop talking.
  • The north-western islands are untouched. Make of that what you will.

Some of what is out there is bigger than your vehicle. A couple of them are bigger than your base. Bring friends, bring rifles, bring a boat you do not mind losing.

Nine skills that level as you play

You earn experience by doing the thing. A skill improves because you used it, not because you spent a point somewhere.

  • Athletics: stamina, sprinting, swimming, climbing, fewer broken bones.
  • Fishing and Hunting: better odds, more meat and fillet, sturdier gear, reading tracks.
  • Metabolism: food and water go further.
  • Stealth: harder to spot, better night sight, silent kills.
  • Strength: carry more, hit harder, wear armour down slower.
  • Survival: fires, cold, foraging, safer mushrooms, hidden stashes.
  • Immunity and Medicine: the two new ones, and they're not decoration. An untreated infection will kill you, and a competent medic is worth travelling for.

Spend experience on perks within each skill. You can respec, but you only get a quarter back. Dying does not cost you experience: what you have learned, you keep.

Skill books are scattered across the island in five grades. Grade one turns up in coastal villages, grade five only in the hardest corners of the map. Fishing books wash up on the coast, hunting books sit in cabins and stands, survival books turn up wherever people actually had to survive. Hold on to anything you don't intend to read: a librarian is coming who pays properly.

The stamina bar is back and weight costs you, but everyone here is still comfortably superhuman from birth. Fire is a roll now, a little under one in three untrained, and failing still teaches you something. Skinning and fishing reward the trained hand with more meat, more pelts and a knife that lasts.

Souls

You start life with three souls. As long as you have one left when you die, you keep your skills and your experience. Spend your last one and the next death takes everything you've learned.

You earn them by killing. A player kill is always worth a soul; AI about a quarter of the time, an animal one in twenty, a zed one in a hundred. Surviving four hours straight also earns one. You can bank twenty-five.

  • The prison: free, with no gear and maybe no shoes.
  • Stonington, Hazelands, Kushville or a random island: 1 soul.
  • Back at your corpse: 2. Your sleeping bag: 3. A Hunters Camp Kit (Hunting or Fishing 20): 5.

In The Underground: Reborn, it pays to kill.

Medicine, and why you want a doctor

Wounds get infected, bruising is tracked, and something you shrug off in the morning can kill you that evening. We've deliberately left the harshest parts off (no amputations, no sleep, no mental state). What stays is the half that makes a medic worth having.

  • Clean hands or gloves count when you treat someone. Dirty hands invite sepsis, and sepsis is the thing that will kill you.
  • A bandaged wound closes in about half an hour. A dirty bandage stretches that out; sterilising wound and dressing speeds it up; a trained medic faster still.
  • Some wounds knock you out instead of killing you. That's a window for somebody to reach you, and you can drag a downed player somewhere defensible first.
  • IFAKs: one item that bandages, restores blood and health in a single action. Found, never bought; the medic trader pays 2,500 for one.
  • Bandages drop far more often, medical zombies carry more, and hospital shelves are stocked again. Use a clean needle before you inject anything.

The world

  • Nine houses you can live in. The RaG cabins are gone; nine different prefab houses replace them, each usable as a base. Normal base building is unchanged.
  • Buried stashes: three times as many places a cache can turn up. Carry a shovel or a pickaxe. Listen for wind chimes.
  • Collectibles: gift boxes hold exactly one collectible each, and there's an interface to see what you're missing. The first person each season to complete the set picks a helicopter.
  • Notoriety is earned through quests and missions. It restricts nothing; it may change what traders charge you, and death doesn't take it. How it works.
  • A quieter map. Fewer missions run at once, so a marker means something again. Clashpoint is paused.
  • Loot boxes now spawn in hospitals, fire stations, industrial areas and stores.
  • Airdrop flares turn up in the world. Find one and you decide where the next drop lands, and who gets to see the smoke.
  • Shooting stars and fireflies. Catch fireflies in a jar and carry them as a light. Eighteen colours; one in a hundred glows purple.
  • Your first spawn comes with a real start: a choice between Car Camp and Base Camp, a codelock, a blade, a bandage and a BK-43. Once per character. Every respawn after that is leaner.

Gear and trade

  • Build your own guns. Weapon benches service worn weapons and build new ones from parts.
  • The recycler breaks gear down into materials and gun parts, takes ruined items, and during the Purge pays out special coins instead.
  • Item rarity shows on every icon, Poor to Mythic. It's information, not a restriction: 7,302 items can be traded.
  • Find it, don't buy it. Nails, saws, hammers, diving gear and a range of tools are no longer sold by traders. They'll still buy them from you, at a premium.
  • Not every trader will deal with you. A handful keep their better stock behind a door you have to earn.
  • Viking gear has arrived and the dragons are hoarding it. Lincoln Abraham, dragon hunter, buys it back, once you've proved you can stand your ground.
  • Blades over maintenance: repair kits are scarcer, knives easier to find. Replace a worn blade rather than nurse it.
  • The costume sets are back: Chucky, Anubis, Godzilla, Buck Hunter, Biker and a few nobody has found yet.
Reopening

Vehicles

  • Bicycles and ATVs spawn in the wild. Bicycles can't be locked, so they're communal. ATVs usually need a battery and a spark plug; STAG-branded ones are in better shape.
  • Abandoned vehicles get impounded after 2 days, to the yard near Waldoboro. Ruined vehicles are only recovered if they were insured. Park in a garage if you're logging off for a while.
  • Garages destroy the old key and issue a new one when you take the vehicle out. Cars, boats and helicopters all use the same system.
  • Stuck? The recovery option is on the Expansion keychain. It can't pull a vehicle out of a garage or the impound.
  • Vehicles are limited to 4 per group, helicopters included.
